# Searching for an edge in crypto markets: pre-registered tests

What a small, slow participant can and cannot earn on liquid crypto markets (spot, options and
perpetual futures, which have no expiry and are kept near the spot price by funding payments between
longs and shorts), tested on public data from Binance, Deribit and Hyperliquid in September and
October 2026. Each study fixed its data, rules, costs, statistics and decision before computing the
statistic the decision rests on, and reported every design it had registered, including those that
failed. Section 1 sets out, study by study, how this was enforced and checked.

> Generated by `python -m cedge.build` from the study outputs in `sources/`. No measured figure in
> this document was typed by hand: each is read from a file listed in `facts.json`. The statements
> the text makes about decisions, signs and agreements between implementations are checked against
> the data when the document is built, and the build fails if a source no longer supports one.
> Protocol parameters, dates and labels are written in the text.

## Summary

| Study | Question | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | Do four rules tied to mechanical flows pay after costs on bitcoin and ether? | none passes |
| Round 2 | Do momentum and carry across the most traded perpetual futures pay, and does a trend rule time spot exposure? | the cross-sectional composite fails; the trend rule passes |
| Round 3 | Does the order-flow rule of round 2 work beyond the top hundred contracts? | fails, inconclusively: not significantly beyond the original rule's returns |
| Dealer gamma | Does option dealers' hedging on Deribit move bitcoin? | no hypothesis passes |
| Volatility | Does a volatility forecast beat implied volatility, and does a weekend option strangle pay? | the forecast fails; the strangle passes |
| Hyperliquid | Do long/short portfolios of Hyperliquid perpetuals, or a machine-learning filter on trend signals, add anything? | rejected |
| Perpetual premia | Do funding carry and time-series momentum beat holding bitcoin, after costs and tax? | both rejected |

Two designs passed: E3, a long-or-cash trend rule on bitcoin and ether spot (round 2), and T1, the
sale of a weekend strangle on bitcoin options, an out-of-the-money call and an out-of-the-money put
sold on Friday and expiring on Sunday (volatility study). Both, and the order-flow rule E4, will be
followed as paper tests on new data (section 9). A pass means that the backtest met the bar its
protocol set in advance; the forward tests are there to check whether the effect persists.

## 1. Method

Every study wrote a protocol before computing the statistic its decision rests on. The protocol
fixes the data, the universe, the rules, the costs, the statistics, the decision rule and the number
of configurations counted as trials. Its SHA-256, a cryptographic hash that changes if any character
of the file changes, ties a run to the text it followed. Beyond that, the safeguards differ by study.

**Rounds 1 to 3 and the volatility study** ran under the same safeguards. Data-quality gates were
computed before the run and written to `gates.json` with the SHA-256 of every input file. The run
records the commit of its code and the SHA-256 of its protocol, and refused to start if it had
already started, if a gate had failed, or if the protocol, the code or a data file differed from
what `gates.json` recorded. Amendments are dated and written into the protocol before the run: none
in round 1, four in round 2, one in round 3 (a contract whose data end early is listed as a known
truncation instead of failing a data gate) and one in the volatility study (pages of the Internet
Archive are downloaded with curl, since the archive refused Python's client). Each `REPORT.md` is
generated from the run's result files.

In these four studies, a second implementation, written separately from the protocol without access
to the study's code, recomputed numbers on the same data:

| Study | Written | Recomputed | Agreement with the run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | after the run | the daily mean return of each rule (the carry, D1, on its bitcoin leg only) and the trade counts | largest difference 0.000009 basis points (bp, hundredths of a percent) a day; same trade counts |
| Round 2 | before the run according to its file's date, but first hashed after the run | the statistics of both decisions, with its own simulations | same decisions; p-values of 0.2395 for the composite and 0.0056 for E3, against 0.2407 and 0.0047; 860 spot checks of its inputs, none differing |
| Round 3 | before the run, and committed | the statistics of the decision | same decision; intercept p-value of 0.1469 against 0.1468; 1,100 spot checks of its inputs, none differing |
| Volatility | before the run, and committed | the four tests | same passing test, T1; p-values of the forecast tests V1 to V3 within 0.0003 of the run's; for T1, the same mean and t-statistic and a bootstrap p-value of 0.0070 against 0.0062 |

The p-values that depend on random draws differ slightly between implementations. The second
implementations of round 3 and of the volatility study were written from the protocol as it stood
before its amendment, which is why their outputs name a different SHA-256 for it
(0f0eff79 and 3d66bfa4, against 862424a5 and 0ec4603c for the
runs; first eight characters).

**The dealer-gamma study** ran under two protocols, each with the same kind of guard against a
second run. The first protocol, amended three times before any test on returns, records its SHA-256
but not the code's commit; the second records both. Its `gates.json` holds the data-quality gates,
and `manifest.json`, written after the first protocol's run, the SHA-256 of every input file. It has
no second implementation. An independent review of the first protocol's results found no error that
changes a decision; the checks and exploratory analyses it asked for are reported in
sections 5 and 6 of the study's `REPORT.md`, labelled as chosen after the results and outside the
decisions.

**The Hyperliquid study** (section 7) registered arc A on 17 September 2026 and committed the
protocol of arcs B and C on 24 September 2026, before any run on real data; checks on synthetic data
ran before the real runs of arcs B and C. The protocol, in French, records the data checks made
before the runs, among them a defect of the price cache that was corrected before the run. It has no
second implementation, and its report, also in French, records no guard against a second run.

**The perpetual-premia study** (section 8) froze its protocol, in French, on 8 September 2026 and was
run on 20 October 2026 from files rebuilt that day from the delivery transcript. It has no
data-quality gates fixed in advance and no second implementation. Its run was started twice: the
first attempt stopped on a data error before printing any statistic.

Interpretation written after a run is kept apart from the generated report, in `POST_RESULTS.md`
(rounds 1 and 2, dealer gamma) and `POST_RESULTS-2.md` (dealer gamma's second protocol), and decides
nothing.

Multiple testing is handled inside each study. Holm's step-down procedure (Holm 1979) keeps the
probability of any false pass among a study's primary designs at the stated level (rounds 1 and 2,
the dealer-gamma hypotheses H1 and H2, the volatility study). The deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and
López de Prado 2014) compares a Sharpe ratio with the maximum expected by chance given the number of
trials; it decides in arc A of section 7 and in section 8, and is reported elsewhere.

## 2. Round 1: rules tied to mechanical flows

Four rules that published work links to predictable flows, on bitcoin and ether: D1, funding carry
(long spot and short the perpetual future, to collect its funding payments); D2, momentum into the
daily 5pm break in Eastern Time (ET, New York); D3, flows into United States spot bitcoin
exchange-traded funds; D4, the reversal after the 3pm to 4pm ET benchmark window, on days the New
York Stock Exchange (NYSE) is open. Prices are Binance's. Every trade is a taker trade, an order that
executes at once against quotes resting in the order book and pays the higher fee: at Binance's fees
for D1 and at Deribit's perpetual fees for the other three. The protocol counted 14
configurations as trials.

| Design | Rule | Window | Sharpe before costs | Net Sharpe | 95% bootstrap interval | Monthly t | One-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Funding carry, bitcoin and ether at equal weight | 2024-04-01 to 2026-09-30 | 5.06 | -1.96 | [-4.84, 1.43] | -1.07 | 0.837 |
| D2 | Momentum into the 5pm ET break, combined rule, bitcoin, weekdays | 2021-01-01 to 2026-09-30 | 0.21 | -1.92 | [-2.74, -1.17] | -5.33 | 1.000 |
| D3 | Spot-fund flows, bitcoin, entry two days after the flow date | 2025-05-01 to 2026-09-30 | -0.15 | -0.42 | [-1.91, 0.84] | -0.72 | 0.736 |
| D4 | Fade of the 3-4pm ET window, bitcoin, NYSE trading days | 2024-01-11 to 2026-09-30 | 0.30 | -2.55 | [-3.53, -1.61] | -6.49 | 1.000 |

No design passes, and every net Sharpe ratio is negative (from -2.55 to -0.42). The carry
shows the pattern most clearly: it earned 0.32 bp a day before costs and paid
0.54 bp a day to trade, so it would break even only if costs fell to 59% of
the level assumed. Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/round1-flows/REPORT.md), interpretation:
[POST_RESULTS.md](sources/round1-flows/POST_RESULTS.md).

## 3. Round 2: slow edges across perpetuals, and trend on spot

The composite combines 21-day momentum and premium carry across the hundred most traded Binance
perpetuals margined in USDT, Tether's dollar stablecoin (532 contracts over the window,
delisted ones included).
E3 is a long-or-cash trend rule on bitcoin and ether spot, judged on its timing: its net return
minus that of a static portfolio holding its average exposures, so that it cannot pass on market
exposure alone.

| Design | Rule | Statistic | Estimate | 95% bootstrap interval | Monthly t | One-sided p | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Composite | Momentum (E1) and premium carry (E2) at inverse-volatility weights | net Sharpe ratio | 0.38 | [-0.50, 1.51] | 0.81 | 0.241 | fail |
| E3 | Trend on bitcoin and ether spot, 30-, 91- and 365-day signals, long or cash | timing information ratio | 0.82 | [0.08, 1.48] | 2.09 | 0.0047 | pass |

The composite's failure is inconclusive: the upper end of its interval, 1.51, does not rule
out a useful Sharpe ratio, so the direction is not closed and the rule was not re-tuned on these data.
E3 passes Holm's test over 69 months. A descriptive design, E4, ranks contracts by taker
order flow, a measure of how much takers bought relative to what they sold (seven-day taker order flow, long the highest, quintiles). It had
a net Sharpe ratio of 1.79 and a deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.97, and kept a positive mean
up to 7.1 times the costs; being descriptive, it decided nothing and was tested again in
round 3.
Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/round2-slow/REPORT.md), interpretation:
[POST_RESULTS.md](sources/round2-slow/POST_RESULTS.md).

## 4. Round 3: order flow beyond the top hundred

The order-flow rule E4, unchanged, on the contracts ranked 101 to 200 by traded volume, from
2023-03-01. To pass, its mean net return had to be significantly positive, and so did its intercept in
a regression of its monthly returns on those of E4 on the top hundred, along with robustness
conditions. The intercept test asks whether the new universe earns more than E4's returns on the top
hundred already explain.

| Design | Net Sharpe | 95% bootstrap interval | Monthly t | One-sided p | Intercept on E4, per month | Intercept t | Intercept p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seven-day taker order flow, long the highest quintile, contracts ranked 101 to 200 | 1.15 | [0.34, 1.96] | 2.80 | 0.017 | 1.91% | 1.34 | 0.147 |

The mean passes its test and the intercept does not, so the study fails, inconclusively. The monthly
correlation with E4 on the top hundred is 0.35. E4 will be followed forward on the top hundred
(section 9). Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/round3-e4b/REPORT.md).

## 5. Deribit dealer gamma and bitcoin returns

An option's gamma is the rate at which its delta, the sensitivity of its value to the underlying
price, changes as that price moves. Dealers who hedge their options must trade the underlying as the
price moves: short gamma, they buy into rises and sell into falls, which should push the price; long
gamma, they do the opposite, which should damp its moves. Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi and Moerke
(2022) find such effects in end-of-day returns of United States stocks, with exchange data that
identify dealers. Deribit does not identify dealers, so the study rebuilt their net gamma hour by hour
from Deribit's option trades on the assumption that the taker of each trade is a customer and the
maker a dealer. It tested three predictions on an estimation sample of 23,376 hours and left the
returns of a later sample unexamined.

| Hypothesis | One-sided p |
|---|---|
| H1, hourly hedging pressure | 0.924 |
| H2, hedging pressure after large moves | 0.784 |
| H3, next-day volatility | 0.320 |
| F1, follow-up on the held-out sample: next-day volatility | 0.502 |
| F2, follow-up on the held-out sample: next-hour reversal when dealers are long gamma | 0.249 |

No hypothesis passes. A second protocol, written after the first failed, tested two follow-ups on the
later sample's 13,152 hours: F1, suggested by an exploratory analysis of the estimation
sample, and F2, from the reference paper's mechanism. Neither passes, and the study ends. Before the
second protocol was written, the share of hours in which dealers were long gamma had been computed
for the years of the later sample (the study's `REPORT.md`, section 5); none of that sample's returns
had been. Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/deribit-gamma/REPORT.md),
[REPORT-2.md](sources/deribit-gamma/REPORT-2.md).

## 6. Volatility forecasts against implied volatility

Three tests asked whether a forecast built from past realized variance and the calendar of scheduled
United States releases knows something that the implied volatility of Deribit's options does not: V1
a predictive regression, V2 a weekly options rule, V3 the pricing of release days. T1 asked whether
the weekend strangle that Deribit had published held up on the weekends its published results did
not cover.

| Test | Estimate | One-sided p |
|---|---|---|
| V1, coefficient of the forecast gap | -0.13 | 0.735 |
| V2, mean weekly return of the rule, bp | -34.2 | 0.991 |
| V3, log ratio of realized to implied variance on release days, minus the same on control days | -0.078 | 0.285 |
| T1, mean weekly return of the weekend strangle, bp | 36.6 | 0.0062 |

V1 to V3 fail: the forecast does not beat the implied volatility. T1 passes over 74
weekends (t = 3.34), winning 81% of them, with the return distribution of an option
seller: a weekly skewness of -2.9, and a worst weekend of -4.4%, the one that began on
2025-10-10. Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/vol-edges/REPORT.md).

## 7. Hyperliquid perpetuals: long/short portfolios and machine-learning filters

Three arcs, on daily data. Arc A is a grid of 54 dollar-neutral long/short portfolios of
Hyperliquid perpetuals, with momentum, reversal, carry and residual momentum signals, by universe size
and weighting. Arcs B and C test meta-labeling (López de Prado 2018): a second model, trained to
predict when a trend signal's trade will pay, filters its trades. Arc B filters Hyperliquid's trend
sleeve, a trend-following portfolio of Hyperliquid perpetuals run at a volatility target; arc C
filters a trend rule on bitcoin and ether spot, on data from 2016.

- Arc A: the best cell (14-day momentum on the 50 contracts with the highest median dollar volume, long the top quintile and short the bottom one; `mom14/50/quintile` in the outputs) has a Sharpe ratio of
  1.34 before costs and 0.24 at 15 bp of costs, and a deflated Sharpe ratio of
  0.032, the probability that its true Sharpe ratio exceeds 1.31, the highest the grid would
  show by chance. It is positive in 2 of its 4 years, and the grid's median
  net Sharpe ratio is -0.63. Rejected.
- Arc B: in all 12 configurations the filter predicts worse than the signal alone, by
  log-loss, the average penalty on predicted probabilities (test p = 0.94 for the primary
  configuration; area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, which measures how well a
  score ranks outcomes, 0.534 against 0.558 for the signal alone). Rejected.
- Arc C: in all 5 configurations the filter predicts worse than the signal alone, by
  log-loss (test p = 0.79 for the primary configuration); the primary filter changes the
  rule's log growth by +2.0% a year (t = 0.38). Rejected.

These rejections rule out a strong effect; an effect of a few percentage points of hit rate could have
gone undetected (ALTS_ML.md, section 5). Full results, in French:
[ALTS_ML.md](sources/hl-alts-ml/ALTS_ML.md).

## 8. Funding carry and time-series momentum on Binance perpetuals

The protocol, written in French and frozen on 8 September 2026, tests two documented premia on
Binance perpetuals margined in USDT against holding bitcoin: delta-neutral funding carry, long spot
and short the perpetual on the contracts with the highest funding (Schmeling, Schrimpf and Todorov
2023), and time-series momentum on the sign of the two-week return, sized to a volatility target
(Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen 2012). It was delivered that day with its code, its tests and a run on
synthetic data, and was not run on real data before 20 October 2026. That day, its files were
rebuilt from the delivery transcript, reproduced the delivery's own checks and were committed before
the run ([PROVENANCE.md](sources/perp-premia/PROVENANCE.md)).

A premium is retained only if its primary configuration meets five criteria: its Sharpe ratio out of
sample (from 2023) exceeds bitcoin's over the same period; the t-statistic of its alpha against
bitcoin out of sample, with Newey-West standard errors (which allow for autocorrelation), is above
two; its deflated Sharpe ratio over the full sample, with the thirteen declared configurations
counted as trials, is at least 0.95; its maximum drawdown stays under 50%; and its annual return
after a 30% tax on positive years is positive. The protocol does not say over which sample the last
two criteria are measured; the code measures them over the full sample.

| Premium | Out-of-sample Sharpe | Bitcoin's, same period | Out-of-sample alpha t | Deflated Sharpe, full sample | Maximum drawdown, full sample | Annual return after tax, full sample | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Funding carry | -0.80 | 1.15 | -1.54 | 0.19 | -17% | +0.5% | rejected |
| Time-series momentum | 0.18 | 1.15 | 0.97 | 0.11 | -15% | +4.2% | rejected |

Both premia are rejected. The carry earned before 2023 and lost afterwards: its primary configuration
had a Sharpe ratio of 2.81 before 2023 and -0.80 after, and the out-of-sample
Sharpe ratio is negative for all nine carry configurations.

The universe is the contracts listed at the time of the run, so delisted contracts are missing: a
survivorship bias that the protocol states in advance and expects to overstate momentum slightly. The
run was started twice. The first attempt stopped before printing any statistic: a paging error in the
collection script had cut every spot price series short, leaving the bitcoin benchmark without values
after 5 June 2022. The script was fixed, spot prices were fetched again and the run was started a
second and last time, with the protocol and the backtest code unchanged. Outputs:
[results.csv](sources/perp-premia/results.csv), [verdict.txt](sources/perp-premia/verdict.txt)
(in French), [protocol](sources/perp-premia/PROTOCOL.md) (in French).

## 10. What the studies say together

1. Costs turned three of round 1's rules from gains into losses: the carry (D1), momentum into the 5pm
   ET break (D2) and the reversal after the benchmark window (D4) have positive Sharpe ratios before
   costs and negative ones after. Costs also took the best long/short cell on Hyperliquid from a
   Sharpe ratio of 1.34 to 0.24.
2. The two published intraday effects tested, D2 and D4, lost money at taker costs in every year of
   their windows.
3. E3's pass measures timing. Its test subtracts the return of a static portfolio holding E3's average
   exposures, so the return that remains comes from when E3 holds bitcoin and ether; E3 still depends
   on the prices of those two coins.
4. E4, taker order flow, had the highest deflated Sharpe ratio of round 2's descriptive designs. Its
   second test, on the contracts ranked 101 to 200, was inconclusive: the mean was significantly
   positive, and the intercept on E4's returns on the top hundred was not.
5. The weekend strangle earned a positive mean with a negatively skewed distribution. Its worst weekend
   began on 2025-10-10, a day the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) calls "the
   largest liquidation day in crypto history" (ESMA 2026).
6. Machine-learning filters built on price, volume and funding variables added nothing detectable: in
   every configuration of arcs B and C, the filter predicted worse than the signal alone. The tests
   could rule out only strong effects (section 7).

## 11. Limitations

- Prices and fees come from a few venues: Binance for rounds 1 to 3 and section 8 (with Deribit's fees
  for three rules of round 1), Deribit's options for sections 5 and 6 (with Binance minute prices for
  realized variance in section 6), Hyperliquid for arcs A and B of section 7 and Coin Metrics for arc
  C. Fees, funding and liquidity differ elsewhere.
- Short samples. Most windows begin between 2021 and 2025; the longest begin in 2016 (arc C of
  section 7) and 2019 (section 8). A rule that passes over a few years may reflect a single market
  regime.
- Fills are simulated from public data at the costs each protocol fixed; market impact beyond those
  costs is not modelled.
- The second implementations were written separately from the protocols, without access to the
  studies' code. They share the protocols' choices of data, costs and windows, so they can find coding
  errors but cannot find a poor choice.
- The studies' decisions are the protocols' decisions. Interpretations written after the runs decide
  nothing.
