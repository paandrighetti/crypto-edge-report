# Searching for an edge in crypto markets: pre-registered tests

What a small, slow participant can and cannot earn on liquid crypto markets, tested on public
data from Binance, Deribit and Hyperliquid in September and October 2026. Each study fixed its
data, rules, costs, statistics and decision before computing the statistic the decision rests on,
ran once, and reported every design it had registered, including those that failed.

> Generated by `python -m cedge.build` from the study outputs in `sources/`. No figure in this
> document was typed by hand: each is read from a file listed in `facts.json`, and the statements
> the text makes about decisions and signs are checked against the data when the document is
> built. If a source changes and one of them no longer holds, the build fails.

(section 9). A pass here says the backtest cleared a stated bar; it does not say the edge persists.

## 1. Method

The five studies run on the server (sections 2 to 6) followed the same discipline:

1. **A protocol written first.** `PREREGISTRATION.md` fixes the data, the universe, the rules, the
   costs, the statistics and the decision, with the number of configurations counted as trials.
   Its SHA-256 and the code's commit are recorded by the run.
2. **Data-quality gates before any return.** The data are collected, checked against published
   checksums where the venue provides them, and gated; the gates are written to `gates.json` before
   the first return is computed.
3. **One run.** The run refuses to start twice or on data that changed since the gates. Amendments,
   when needed, were written before the run and dated.
4. **An independent check.** For rounds 1 to 3 and the volatility study, a second implementation,
   written from the protocol without the study's code, recomputed the decisional numbers on the same
   data, and the differences are reported. The dealer-gamma study had an independent review of its
   results instead.
5. **Generated reports.** Each study's `REPORT.md` is rendered from its result files; interpretation
   written after the run is kept apart, in `POST_RESULTS.md`, and decides nothing.

Sections 7 and 8 are lighter: each had its protocol committed before the first run on real data and
a synthetic check first, but no data gates and no independent recomputation.

Multiple testing is handled inside each study: Holm's step-down procedure across the primary designs,
and the deflated Sharpe ratio (Bailey and López de Prado 2014), which compares a Sharpe ratio with
the maximum expected by chance given the number of trials.

## 2. Round 1: rules tied to mechanical flows

Four rules that published work links to predictable flows, on bitcoin and ether: delta-neutral
funding carry, momentum into the daily 5pm ET (New York) break, United States spot-fund flows, and
the reversal after the 3pm to 4pm ET benchmark window. Every trade is a taker trade, at the venues'
fee schedules. 14 configurations counted as trials.

| Design | Rule | Window | Sharpe before costs | Net Sharpe | 95% bootstrap interval | Monthly t | One-sided p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | Funding carry, bitcoin and ether at equal weight | 2024-04-01 to 2026-09-30 | 5.06 | -1.96 | [-4.84, 1.43] | -1.07 | 0.837 |
| D2 | Momentum into the 5pm ET break, combined rule, bitcoin, weekdays | 2021-01-01 to 2026-09-30 | 0.21 | -1.92 | [-2.74, -1.17] | -5.33 | 1.000 |
| D3 | Spot-fund flows, bitcoin, entry two days after the flow date | 2025-05-01 to 2026-09-30 | -0.15 | -0.42 | [-1.91, 0.84] | -0.72 | 0.736 |
| D4 | Fade of the 3-4pm ET window, bitcoin, NYSE trading days | 2024-01-11 to 2026-09-30 | 0.30 | -2.55 | [-3.53, -1.61] | -6.49 | 1.000 |

No design passes, and every net Sharpe ratio is negative (from -2.55 to -0.42). The carry
shows the pattern most clearly: it earned 0.32 basis points (bp) a day before costs and
paid 0.54 bp a day to trade, so it breaks even only at 59% of the assumed
costs. Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/round1-flows/REPORT.md), interpretation:
[POST_RESULTS.md](sources/round1-flows/POST_RESULTS.md).

## 3. Round 2: slow edges across perpetuals, and trend on spot

The composite combines 21-day momentum and premium carry across the hundred most traded
USDT-margined Binance perpetuals (532 contracts over the window, delisted ones included).
E3 is a long-or-cash trend rule on bitcoin and ether spot, judged on its timing: its net return
minus that of a static portfolio holding its average exposures, so that it cannot pass on market
exposure alone.

| Design | Rule | Statistic | Estimate | 95% bootstrap interval | Monthly t | One-sided p | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Composite | Momentum (E1) and premium carry (E2) at inverse-volatility weights | net Sharpe ratio | 0.38 | [-0.50, 1.51] | 0.81 | 0.241 | fail |
| E3 | Trend on bitcoin and ether spot, 30-, 91- and 365-day signals, long or cash | timing information ratio | 0.82 | [0.08, 1.48] | 2.09 | 0.0047 | pass |

The composite's failure is inconclusive: the upper end of its interval, 1.51, does not rule
out a useful Sharpe ratio, so the direction is not closed and the rule was not re-tuned on these data.
E3 passes Holm's test over 69 months. A descriptive design, E4 (Seven-day taker order flow, long the highest, quintiles), had a net
Sharpe ratio of 1.79 and a deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.97, and kept a positive mean up
to 7.1 times the costs; being descriptive, it decided nothing and was tested again in round 3.
Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/round2-slow/REPORT.md), interpretation:
[POST_RESULTS.md](sources/round2-slow/POST_RESULTS.md).

## 4. Round 3: order flow beyond the top hundred

The order-flow rule E4, unchanged, on the contracts ranked 101 to 200 by traded volume, from
2023-03-01. To pass, it had to earn a positive mean and a positive intercept against E4's own returns
on the top hundred: a new universe that only repeats the old rule's returns adds no evidence.

| Design | Net Sharpe | 95% bootstrap interval | Monthly t | One-sided p | Intercept on E4, per month | Intercept t | Intercept p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seven-day taker order flow, long the highest quintile, contracts ranked 101 to 200 | 1.15 | [0.34, 1.96] | 2.80 | 0.017 | 1.91% | 1.34 | 0.147 |

The mean passes its test; the intercept does not, so the study fails, inconclusively. The monthly
correlation with E4 on the top hundred is 0.35. The rule now runs forward on new months
(section 9). Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/round3-e4b/REPORT.md).

## 5. Deribit dealer gamma and bitcoin returns

If option dealers hedge their gamma, their trades should push bitcoin when they are short gamma and
damp its moves when they are long, as end-of-day hedging does in United States equities (Barbon,
Beckmeyer, Buraschi and Moerke 2022). The study rebuilt dealers' net gamma hour by hour from
Deribit's option trades and tested three predictions on an estimation sample of 23,376 hours,
keeping a later sample unexamined.

| Hypothesis | One-sided p |
|---|---|
| H1, hourly hedging pressure | 0.924 |
| H2, hedging pressure after large moves | 0.784 |
| H3, next-day volatility | 0.320 |
| F1, follow-up on the held-out sample: next-day volatility | 0.502 |
| F2, follow-up on the held-out sample: next-hour reversal when dealers are long gamma | 0.249 |

No hypothesis passes. A second, pre-registered protocol tested two follow-ups on the held-out
13,152 hours; neither passes, and the study ends. Full results:
[REPORT.md](sources/deribit-gamma/REPORT.md), [REPORT-2.md](sources/deribit-gamma/REPORT-2.md).

## 6. Volatility forecasts against implied volatility

Three tests asked whether a forecast built from past realized variance and scheduled United States
releases knows something that Deribit's implied volatility does not (V1 a predictive regression, V2 a
weekly options rule, V3 implied volatility on release days). T1 asked whether a weekend strangle
that Deribit had published, sold on Friday and expiring on Sunday, held up on the weekends its
published results did not cover.

| Test | Estimate | One-sided p |
|---|---|---|
| V1, coefficient of the forecast gap | -0.13 | 0.735 |
| V2, mean weekly return of the rule, bp | -34.2 | 0.991 |
| V3, log realized over implied variance on release days | -0.078 | 0.285 |
| T1, mean weekly return of the weekend strangle, bp | 36.6 | 0.0062 |

V1 to V3 fail: the forecast does not beat the implied volatility. T1 passes over 74
weekends (t = 3.34), winning 81% of them, with the shape expected of an option seller:
weekly skewness of -2.9, and the worst weekend, -4.4%, on the one that began on
2025-10-10. Full results: [REPORT.md](sources/vol-edges/REPORT.md).

## 7. Hyperliquid perpetuals: alts long/short and machine-learning filters

A dollar-neutral long/short grid across Hyperliquid's alts (54 cells: momentum, reversal,
carry and residual momentum, by universe size and weighting), and a meta-labeling filter (López de
Prado 2018) trained to predict when a trend signal is right, first on Hyperliquid's trend sleeve,
then on a bitcoin and ether trend rule.

- Long/short: the best cell, mom14/50/quintile, has a Sharpe ratio of 1.34 before costs and
  0.24 net at 15 bp of costs, and a deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.032: the probability that its
  true Sharpe ratio exceeds 1.31, the best the grid would show by chance; 2/4 years positive. The grid's median net Sharpe ratio is -0.63. Rejected.
- Meta-labeling the trend sleeve: in all 12 configurations the filter predicts worse than
  the signal alone (log-loss test p = 0.94 for the primary one; area under the ROC curve
  0.534 against 0.558 for the signal). Rejected.
- Meta-labeling the bitcoin and ether trend rule: log-loss test p = 0.79, growth difference
  +2.0% a year (t = 0.38). Rejected.

Full results (in French): [ALTS_ML.md](sources/hl-alts-ml/ALTS_ML.md).

## 9. What runs forward

None of these rules is traded. Each runs as a paper test frozen before its window starts, with
looks fixed in advance:

- **E3**, the trend rule, from 1 November 2026, looks in November 2028 and November 2030.
- **E4**, the order-flow rule, on the hundred most traded contracts from 1 November 2026, looks in
  November 2028 and, if that look decides nothing, November 2030.
- **T1**, the weekend strangle, from Friday 30 October 2026, looks on costs in January 2027 and on
  returns in October 2027 and October 2028.

## 10. What the studies say together

1. **Costs decide.** Several designs earn something before costs and lose after: the carry, the two
   intraday rules, the best alts long/short cell. A rule that turns its book over daily needs a gross edge most
   retail-accessible signals do not have.
2. **Published intraday effects did not survive.** The 5pm ET momentum and the benchmark-window
   reversal lost money at taker costs in every year.
3. **Trend survives as timing, not as alpha.** E3 passes a test that strips its average exposure, but
   what it buys is better timing of bitcoin and ether, not a return independent of them.
4. **The strongest signal is order flow, and it did not travel.** E4 is the best descriptive design
   of round 2; on the next hundred contracts it earns a positive mean, but not significantly more than
   the original rule's returns explain.
5. **Option selling pays a premium with a tail.** The weekend strangle's mean is positive and its worst
   weekend is the one that began on 2025-10-10, the day of the October 2025 crash.
6. **Machine-learning filters on price data added nothing.** Every configuration predicted worse than
   the signal it was meant to filter.

## 11. Limitations

- One venue per study: Binance for rounds 1 to 3, Deribit for options, Hyperliquid for alts. Fees,
  funding and liquidity differ elsewhere.
- Short samples in calendar time, at most six years, all in one crypto cycle. A pass can be a regime.
- Fills are simulated at taker costs from public data; market impact beyond the quoted spread is not
  modelled.
- The independent recomputations were written by a separate agent from the protocols. They check the
  code against the protocol, not the protocol's choices.
- The studies' decisions are the protocols' decisions. The interpretations in each `POST_RESULTS.md`
  were written after the runs and decide nothing.
